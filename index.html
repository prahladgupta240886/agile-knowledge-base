Scrum Core 01

Core Agile & Scrum Foundations

Agile Philosophy

A high-level project management philosophy emphasizing flexibility, incremental delivery, customer feedback, and continuous collaborative improvement.

Scrum Framework

Scrum is a specific agile framework that provides structured approach with role, events, and artifacts to manage project work in short cycle called sprints.

Three Empirical Pillars of Scrum

1. Transparency Process and state must be visible to those managing and executing work.
2. Inspection Frequent cadence to check progress toward sprint objectives and detect variance.
3. Adaptation Prompt adjustment of processes or materials when deviations occur.

6 Scrum Principles

  • Empirical Process Control: Observation & experimentation over rigid planning
  • Self-Organization: Teams define the path to goals
  • Collaboration: Continuous joint problem-solving
  • Value-Based Prioritization: Business value drives order
  • Time-Boxing: Fixed limits for all activities
  • Iterative Development: Incremental refinements

Refinement 03

User Story Principle: INVEST (By Bill Wake)

A foundational mnemonic guide created by Bill Wake to evaluate the quality of user stories before entering sprint commitment.

I

Independent

Standalone stories that can be worked on without waiting for other tasks/stories.

N

Negotiable

Serves as a prompt for conversation - details remain open to adjustment with the Product Owner as clarity improves.

V

Valuable

Must provide meaningful, measurable value to end-users, customers, or business stakeholders.

E

Estimable

Sufficiently clear in scope to allow team members to size complexity and relative effort reliably.

S

Small

Sized appropriately to easily finish within a single sprint timeframe without rolling over.

T

Testable

Explicit acceptance criteria enabling automated or manual QA pass/fail validation.

Refinement 04

Product Backlog Refinement & Prioritization

Core Objective: The ongoing process of preparing backlog items for sprint planning through clarification, prioritization, and relative estimation to ensure they are development-ready.

1. Detailing & Clarification

  • User Story Refinement: Breakdown into smaller functional increments.
  • Requirement Clarity: Shared stakeholder understanding.
  • Acceptance Criteria: Objective pass/fail bounds.
  • Dependencies: External blockers proactively resolved.
  • Progressive Elaboration: Refine backlog as items move up in priority.

2. Prioritization Frameworks

  • Value-Driven: Ordering items based on business value.
  • MoSCoW: Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have.
  • Stack Ranking: Strict 1-to-N serial priority list.
  • Kano Model: Evaluate item based on customer satisfaction and feature importance.
  • Cost of Delay: Weighing based on unit of delay.

3. Estimation & Sizing

  • Planning Poker: Consensus-driven story sizing using playing cards.
  • T-Shirt Sizing: High-level bucket sizing (XS, S, M, L, XL).
  • Fibonacci Sequence: Exponential scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) reflecting uncertainty.
  • Relative Sizing: Sizing items comparatively against a baseline story.
  • Affinity Mapping: Rapidly grouping stories into shared effort categories.

4. Backlog Management

  • Regular refinement cadence meetings
  • Manage emergent items(add/remove/change) based on new insight or requirements
  • Aggressively eliminate duplicate stories
  • Decompose large Epics into sprint stories
  • Remove outdated or irrelevant items
Refinement 05

Agile & User Story Estimation Techniques

1. Planning Poker

Consensus-based gamified sizing where cards are revealed simultaneously to prevent anchoring bias.

2. T-Shirt Sizing

XS, S, M, L, XL categorization designed for quick, coarse-grained estimation during roadmapping.

3. Affinity Mapping

Writing stories on cards, clustering by dependencies and similarities, then assigning group effort.

4. Story Point Estimation

Abstract relative metrics combining volume, complexity, risk, and technical effort.

5. Bucket System

Sorting large batches of user stories rapidly into predefined buckets of estimated complexity.

6. Fibonacci Series

1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21... Reflects escalating uncertainty as task size increases.

Quality Gates 06

Quality Gates: Definition of Ready (DoR) vs. Definition of Done (DoD)

Definition of Ready (DoR)

8 criteria
  • Well-defined scope
  • Acceptance criteria documented
  • No external dependencies blocking work
  • Task estimation completed
  • Prioritized in the backlog
  • Small user story meeting INVEST criteria
  • Resource and skill requirements identified
  • Stakeholder communication & approval secured
Key Benefits: Minimizes sprint spillover, guarantees clear acceptance targets, prevents context thrashing, and boosts team alignment.

Definition of Done (DoD)

11 criteria
  • 1. Code completion finalized
  • 2. Working functionality verified
  • 3. Performance optimization executed
  • 4. Peer code review approved
  • 5. Unit & QA test passed successfully
  • 6. Acceptance criteria fully satisfied
  • 7. User & system documentation updated
  • 8. Build pipeline completed successfully
  • 9. Code merged to target branch
  • 10. Stakeholder communication concluded
  • 11. Sign-off and approval by PM / PO
Retrospectives 07

Popular Sprint Retrospective Techniques

Start, Stop, Continue

Action-oriented framework for new initiatives to start, bad habits to stop, and winning habits to retain.

Mad, Sad, Glad

Emotional safety model categorizing team feelings (frustrations, disappointments, and celebrations).

Mountain Climber

Mostly useful for challenging sprints. This technique uses the metaphor of a mountain climber to analyze roadblocks faced along the way and celebrate team progress.

Rose, Bud, Thorn

Rose: What went well.
Bud: Opportunities.
Thorn: Pain point or obstacle.

Hot Air Balloon

Combines retrospective reflection (hot air/sandbags) with futurespective visioning.

4 Ls

Loved, Learned, Lacked, and Longed For.

Movie Critique

Creative, gamified sprint debriefing conducted like reviewing a feature film release.

Classic 3 Questions

1. What went well?
2. What did not go well?
3. What can be improved?

Agile KPIs & Scrum Metrics

Agile KPIs (Executive Level)

Customer Satisfaction:

Measures end-user sentiment and retention via CSAT/NPS across shipped releases.

Time to Market:

Duration from initial concept formulation to user-facing production release.

Return on Investment (ROI):

Economic returns achieved relative to engineering capital expenditure.

Product Quality:

Quantified by defect density and escaped bugs discovered in production.

Business Value Delivered:

Measurable real-world impact and functional capability delivered.

Scrum Operational Metrics

Velocity:

Average story points accepted per sprint, measuring delivery capacity over time.

Sprint Burndown:

Graph visualizing work remaining vs. ideal timeline to predict sprint goal attainment.

Commitment vs. Completion Rate:

Percentage of planned work accepted at sprint review (reliability baseline).

Lead Time:

Total duration from request intake to final production customer release.

Cycle Time:

Active elapsed development time from work start until completion.

SAFe 6.0 09

Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe): Overview & 10 Principles

What is SAFe? A proven framework for large, complex enterprise organizations seeking to scale agile practices across multi-tiered teams, aligning hundreds of engineers around a synchronized business rhythm.

1. Improved Alignment

Align teams and stakeholders around common goal.

2. Increased Productivity

Delivers working software in predictable cadence.

3. Enhanced Quality

Integrate quality into every aspect of development.

4. Better Decisions

Prioritizes initiatives strictly based on economic benefits.

The 10 SAFe Principles

#1 Take an economic view Base decisions on economic outcomes by evaluating sequence, batch sizes, and Cost of Delay.
#2 Apply systems thinking View the entire system of work including workers & users, to optimize the whole rather than just individual parts.
#3 Assume variability; preserve options Embrace change and uncertainty by using set based design to keep options open and remain flexible.
#4 Build incrementally with fast learning cycles Develop the solution in small frequent increments with built in feedback loops to learn and adapt quickly.
#5 Base milestones on objective evaluation of working systems Measure progress against working software and tangible systems rather than speculative design documents.
#6 Make value flow without interruptions Visualize and limit WIP, shrink batch sizes, and optimize queues.
#7 Apply cadence, synchronize with cross-domain planning Use a regular cadence (a heart beat) to synchronize activities and planning across different teams and domains for better alignment.
#8 Unlock intrinsic motivation of knowledge workers Empower teams through autonomy, mastery, purpose, and trust.
#9 Decentralize decision-making Empower teams to make routine decisions quickly, reserving centralization only for rare, strategic choices.
#10 Organize around value Structure teams around end-to-end value streams rather than department silos.

SAFe 6.0 11

SAFe Ceremonies, Artifacts & ROAM Governance

PI Planning (Program Increment Planning)

Cadence Event • 8–12 Weeks

The heartbeat of the Agile Release Train (ART). A routine two-day planning event where all cross-functional teams face-to-face (or virtually) align to a shared vision, discover dependencies, and commit to a set of PI Objectives.

• Committed PI Objectives: Business and technical goals agreed upon by teams and business owners.
• ART Program Board: Visual map of features, cross-team dependencies, and delivery milestones.

ROAM Board (PI Planning Risk Management)

Risk Governance

During PI Planning, program-level risks identified by individual teams are surfaced to the entire ART and categorized onto the ROAM board to ensure full transparency and accountability.

R — Resolved Closed

The risk has been completely addressed and no longer poses any threat to the project or delivery timeline. No further action is required.

Action: None required.
O — Owned Assigned

Cannot be resolved immediately during planning. A specific individual or team member ("the owner") accepts responsibility for tracking and managing it post-session.

Action: Must have a named assignee.
A — Accepted Tolerated

The team acknowledges the risk exists but decides to live with it as-is—typically because probability or impact is very low, or the cost of fixing it outweighs the damage.

Action: Acknowledged without fixing.
M — Mitigated Action Plan

The team creates and implements a plan of action to reduce the likelihood of the risk occurring or to minimize its impact if it does happen.

Action: Execute reduction plan.

Key ART Synchronizations & Inspection Events

Scrum of Scrums (SoS)

Weekly RTE-facilitated sync for Scrum Masters to tackle impediments and cross-team bottlenecks.

PO Sync

Regular sync between Product Management and POs to review feature progress and scope trade-offs.

System Demo

Bi-weekly integrated demonstration of new features across all teams in a staging environment.

Inspect & Adapt (I&A)

End-of-PI retrospective and structured problem-solving workshop to address root causes.
